> I was a hidden treasure, and I loved to be known.
>
> — hadith qudsi

> A heap of broken images.
>
> — T. S. Eliot, *The Waste Land*

This is not a garden.

A garden is tended. Things are planted where they will be seen, pruned when
they sprawl, arranged so that a visitor can walk through and come away with
something. Everything here is the opposite of that.

What gets published is a screenshot: a handful of notes held on one screen at
one moment, exactly as they sat there. No caption, no commentary, no tidying.
Each one is named by five characters that mean nothing, and dated by the day it
surfaced rather than the day it was written — so the order you find them in
tells you nothing about the order they were thought.

There is no index and no chronology. The only structure is a graph, and every
line in it was inferred by a machine reading the fragments; none was drawn on
purpose. Whatever pattern you see is one you brought with you, or one that was
already there.

That is the whole experiment. A heap of broken images is closer to what
thinking looks like than any essay written afterwards about the thinking.
Publishing the heap instead of the essay is the only thing being attempted
here.
